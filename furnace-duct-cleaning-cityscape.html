Furnace and Duct Cleaning for Cityscape Homes

Cityscape began around 2014 and built out through the rest of that decade, sitting between Country Hills Boulevard and Stoney Trail with Redstone and Cornerstone to the north and Skyview Ranch to the west. Most homes are between five and twelve years old.

That is an interesting age. Old enough that the pure construction phase is over and normal household dust has started accumulating, but young enough that whatever the builder left in the ductwork is still in there underneath it. We often find both on the same job: a layer of drywall dust from 2016 with five or six years of ordinary living settled on top.

Cityscape also has a high proportion of first-time buyers and young families, which shows up in the systems. More occupancy per square foot, more cooking, more laundry, and often a pet in a home that is not large. All of it loads the cold air return faster than the square footage alone would suggest.

How Housing Type Changes the Job Here

Townhome blocks

Compact systems, often eight to fourteen vents, with the furnace in a small mechanical closet rather than an open basement. Duct runs are short and vertical. These are quick jobs and priced well below a detached home, which is worth knowing because some companies quote a flat residential rate regardless.

Semi-detached and laned homes

Narrow and deep, so the trunk runs front to back with short branches off it, and the mechanical room is usually tight against a party wall. Fifteen to twenty-two vents is typical. Rear-lane garages mean people come in across a gravel lane and a back yard, which puts more grit into the return than a front-driveway house.

Detached two-storey

The largest systems in the community, twenty to thirty vents, often with a developed or partly developed basement. These take the full two to four hours and are where the difference is most visible when we open the system up.

HRVs throughout

Standard in this build era. Separate ducting, a core and two filters that most owners have never touched. In a townhome the HRV is frequently tucked somewhere awkward and has been ignored entirely since possession.

Flex duct on final runs

Common on upper-floor registers here as in the neighbouring communities. Ribbed interior, holds dust, tears under an aggressive brush. We check before we start rather than after.

Basements finished later

Very common in Cityscape as budgets allowed. If the furnace ran during drywall sanding, and it nearly always did, that dust is through the entire return side. This is the single most frequent reason we get called here.
