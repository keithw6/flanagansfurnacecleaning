Furnace and Duct Cleaning for Cornerstone Homes

Cornerstone broke ground around 2016 and has been building continuously since, working outward toward Stoney Trail and 60 Street NE. Some streets have been occupied for years. Others took possession last season, and further phases are still framing.

Every house here is new, so what is in the ductwork is construction material rather than household dust. Drywall dust is the main one, and it gets everywhere: it is fine enough to coat every interior surface of the system and it stays there because nothing in a duct disturbs it except airflow. We also routinely pull out sawdust, insulation fibre, screws, zip tie offcuts, poly scraps and rubbish left by trades.

The builder walkthrough usually includes a claim that the ducts were cleaned. In practice that generally means the register openings were vacuumed so they looked clean on the day. Pull a register and run a brush down the branch and the difference is not subtle.

The Timing Question, Which Matters More Here

Cornerstone is the one community where we most often tell people to wait, and it is worth explaining why rather than just saying it.

If your street is still being built out, dust is arriving continuously. Framing, cutting, grading, street sweeping and the general churn of an active construction site put fine particulate into the air, and it comes into your house on shoes, through open windows in summer, and through the fresh air intake on your HRV. Cleaning a system while that is happening around you means cleaning it again later.

Wait if

  • Lots on your street or the next street are still being framed
  • Your basement is not yet developed
  • You took possession within the last year
  • Trades are still returning for warranty work

Book it now if

  • Your phase is complete and the street is quiet
  • Basement development is finished
  • You took possession two or more years ago and have never had it done
  • Someone in the house has asthma, significant allergies, or there is a newborn

Call and describe where your phase is at. If the answer is wait another season, that is what you will hear, and we would rather have that conversation than take a booking that has to be repeated.

What Cornerstone Systems Look Like Inside

Modern sealed furnaces

Condensing high-efficiency units, often two-stage or modulating, with ECM blower motors. Excellent equipment, but tighter tolerances mean restricted airflow shows up faster. A blower wheel carrying construction dust is worth dealing with before it becomes a performance complaint.

HRVs as standard

Most Cornerstone homes have a heat recovery ventilator with its own ducting, a core and two filters. If yours has never been opened, and in a house this new it very likely has not, it is probably running well below its rated airflow. We check them.

Flex on the upper runs

Insulated flexible duct on final runs to upper-floor registers is common in this community. It traps dust more readily than rigid pipe and it tears if brushed aggressively. We identify what you have before starting.

High-density housing mix

Cornerstone has a lot of townhome blocks, semi-detached and laned product alongside the detached homes. Smaller systems, fewer vents, tighter mechanical rooms, and priced accordingly rather than on a one-size figure.

Builder-grade filters

One inch filters in a slot beside the furnace. In a brand-new home with residual construction dust these load remarkably fast. Every two to three months for the first couple of years, then you can relax the schedule.

Air conditioning

More common in new builds than in older Calgary stock. The evaporator coil sits directly in the airflow above the furnace and restricts air the same way a dirty filter does, so it is worth a look during a cleaning.
