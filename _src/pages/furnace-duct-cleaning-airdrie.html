Three Eras of Airdrie Housing

1980s and 1990s

Edwards, Meadowbrook, Fairways, Big Springs, Summerhill and the older central streets. These systems are thirty to forty years old with everything that implies: heavily loaded returns, blower wheels furred solid with pet hair, and usually a basement developed somewhere along the way.

Original furnaces are long gone. What we usually see is a modern replacement fitted into ductwork sized for the older unit.

2000s family communities

Luxstone, Willowbrook, Ravenswood, King's Heights, Sagewood, Reunion, Prairie Springs and Windsong. Fifteen to twenty-five years old, the sweet spot where a first cleaning is well overdue and the before and after is genuinely visible.

Developed basements are the norm rather than the exception, and that drywall dust is still in the return side.

The newest subdivisions

Bayside, Cooper's Crossing, Hillcrest, Chinook Gate, South Windsong, Wildflower and the phases still being framed. Construction debris rather than household dust: drywall, sawdust, insulation fibre, screws and poly offcuts.

If your street is still being built, call and ask before booking. We may tell you to wait a season.

What Airdrie Systems Have in Common

Two things run across every era of housing here, and both come down to where the town sits.

Wind, constantly

Airdrie is exposed. It sits on open prairie north of Calgary with very little to break the wind, and residents will tell you it blows more here than in the city. That matters for ductwork because wind keeps fine soil airborne and drives it into houses through every opening and intake.

Homes on the western and northern edges take the most of it, and shoulder-season open windows bring a steady supply indoors.

Continuous construction

Airdrie has been building without pause for two decades. Even in an established community, there is often an active phase or a new subdivision within a kilometre or two, generating grading dust and truck traffic. It is not a reason to avoid cleaning, but it is a reason the interval here is often shorter than you would expect.

Three to four years suits most Airdrie households better than five.

Newer Airdrie builds also come with HRVs, flexible duct on some upper runs, sealed high-efficiency furnaces with ECM blowers, and thin builder-grade filters that clog fast. All of it is covered on the indoor air quality page, including which filter upgrades are worth the money.
