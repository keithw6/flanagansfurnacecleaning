Complete Duct Cleaning for Calgary Homes & Businesses

Duct cleaning has a bad reputation in this city, and it was earned. The eighty-nine dollar whole-home special is a real business model: a crew arrives, runs a shop vacuum down four or five accessible vents, then explains that your system has mould, that the price only covered the main trunk, and that the actual job will be six hundred dollars. Plenty of Calgary homeowners have lived through exactly that.

We quote one number for the whole system. That number covers every supply line, every return, every register, and the trunk, regardless of how many vents turn up once we start counting. If your house has thirty vents instead of the twenty you estimated on the phone, that is our estimating error, not your invoice problem.

What gets cleaned on a duct cleaning

  • Main supply trunk, brushed and vacuumed end to end
  • Every supply branch line running off the trunk
  • Every cold air return line back to the furnace
  • All registers and grilles removed, cleaned, and refitted
  • Return air plenum and the furnace-side connections
  • Blower compartment, because it is where the dust collects
  • New standard furnace filter installed

Add-ons like dryer vent cleaning or system sanitization are quoted separately and only when they make sense.

The signs your ducts actually need it

Dust settling back on furniture within a day of cleaning. Visible fuzz around the register grilles. A basement development or renovation finished in the last year. A newly built home, particularly a possession within the last two years. Pets that shed. Someone in the house with asthma or allergies that get worse indoors in winter.

If none of those apply and your last cleaning was two years ago, you probably do not need us yet, and we will say so on the phone rather than book the job.

Residential Duct Cleaning Calgary

Most Calgary houses are forced-air with a sheet metal trunk in the basement and round branch lines feeding each room. What varies is the age of the stock, and it changes how the job runs.

Newer builds, 2005 onward

Redstone, Cornerstone, Mahogany, Seton and the rest of the outer ring. These systems are usually clean metal but carry construction debris that was never removed: drywall dust, sawdust, screws, and offcuts of poly. Some builders run flex duct on the last stretch to a register, which needs a gentler brush and more care.

Established stock, 1975 to 2000

Much of Taradale, Coventry Hills, McKenzie Towne and the inner NE. Twenty to fifty years of accumulated dust, and often a developed basement added at some point which put a large volume of drywall dust into the return. These are the systems where the difference is most visible.

Older inner-city homes

Pre-1975 stock in the inner NE and NW sometimes still has original octopus-style ductwork, oversized trunks, or asbestos-wrapped sections on very old systems. We identify those before starting and will not disturb wrapping. That is a specialist abatement job, not a duct cleaning, and we will tell you so.

A typical house takes between two and four hours depending on size and how many vents there are. Two-storey homes with a developed basement sit at the longer end. We do not rush a job to fit three more in that day.

Commercial Duct Cleaning Calgary

Offices, retail units, restaurants, daycares, small warehouses and multi-unit residential buildings. Commercial systems are larger, often rooftop-mounted, and usually need to be cleaned outside business hours so the space stays usable.

How commercial work differs

Rooftop units, longer trunk runs, multiple zones and often a maintenance schedule tied to a lease or a health inspection. We walk the system and quote from what is actually there rather than a square footage formula.

Evening and weekend scheduling is normal for these jobs and does not carry a premium.

How Our Duct Cleaning Works

  1. Walk the system

    We count the vents, find the returns, check the trunk layout and note anything unusual before a single piece of equipment comes off the truck.

  2. Put the system under negative pressure

    The vacuum connects at the trunk so that everything we loosen has one direction to travel, and it is not into your living room.

  3. Brush every line individually

    Rotary brush down each branch, working the debris back toward the trunk. Registers come off so the last few feet get done properly, which is the section a hose-in-the-vent cleaning always misses.

  4. Returns, blower, then filter

    The return side is cleaned the same way, the blower compartment gets vacuumed out, and a new filter goes in.

  5. You look at what came out

    Before we pack up. It is the most convincing part of the job and it costs us nothing to show you.

Debris travels one way only: down the branch, into the trunk, out to the vacuum. Nothing gets pushed further into the house.
