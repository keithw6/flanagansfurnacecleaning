Furnace and Duct Cleaning for New Brighton Homes

New Brighton built out between roughly 2003 and 2012, east of 52 Street SE around New Brighton Drive and Brightonwoods, with the residents' club at its centre. It went up alongside Copperfield next door in the same wave of southeast expansion.

This is family housing: two-storey detached, attached and rear-lane garages, plus a good number of townhomes. Fifteen to twenty-two years in, most of these systems have never been cleaned, and a large share have had a basement developed at some point along the way.

The result is a straightforward accumulation job rather than the construction-debris work we do further south in Mahogany and Seton. Ordinary household dust, pet hair, cooking residue and soil, plus whatever the basement development contributed.

What Fifteen to Twenty Years Puts in a System

Loaded blower wheels

Pet hair and fine dust packed between the fins until the wheel is effectively solid. It moves noticeably less air than it should, and it is the part of the system nobody has ever seen. Showing you yours is usually the moment the job explains itself.

Basement drywall dust

Most New Brighton basements were developed after possession, plenty of them during the 2010s. Sanding drywall with the furnace running puts an enormous volume of fine dust through the return side in a couple of days, and it stays there.

Heavy return side

Returns pull everything the household produces toward the furnace, so they load faster than the supply side. They are also the part a cheap cleaning most often skips, because they are less visible and less convenient.

Neglected humidifiers

Nearly every home of this era has one and a large share are not working: pads scaled solid, water feeds shut off and forgotten, or the bypass damper stuck in the summer position. We check and can replace a pad while we are there.

Untouched exhaust runs

Bathroom and kitchen exhaust ducts in homes this age have almost always never been cleaned. Same for the dryer vent, which is the one that actually matters for fire risk.

Second and third owners

Common here now. If nobody could tell you when the system was last cleaned, the honest working assumption is never, and the system is carrying somebody else's household along with yours.

Just Bought in New Brighton?

A good share of our work here is new owners in the first few months, and it is a sensible time to do it.

You have no history on the system. If the previous owners had pets and you do not, or the reverse, the ductwork is carrying their household. If anyone ever smoked in the house, that residue is in there and it re-releases every time the blower runs.

One proper cleaning at the start gives you a known baseline, after which three to five years is fine for most households. The combined furnace and duct cleaning is what we would suggest for a first pass, since the blower wants dealing with as much as the pipe does.
