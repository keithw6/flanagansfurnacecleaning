What Goes Wrong With Central Vacuums

A central vacuum is a good system. The power unit sits in the garage or mechanical room, rigid PVC tubing runs through the walls to inlets in each room, and the dirt ends up in a canister far away from where you are cleaning. Because it is out of sight, it gets ignored for years at a time.

Suction has dropped off

Usually a full canister or a clogged filter, sometimes a partial blockage in the tubing, occasionally a motor nearing the end of its life. These have different fixes and different costs, so identifying which one it is matters.

Something is stuck in the line

Socks, LEGO, coins, hair elastics, pen lids, dog toys. It happens in every household with children. A blockage at a bend can usually be cleared from the inlet or the power unit end without opening a wall.

The system smells

Fine dust builds up along the tubing walls over the years, and in a system that has moved anything damp it can hold odour. Cleaning the tubing itself deals with this properly rather than masking it.

An inlet does not work

Often the low-voltage contact in the inlet valve rather than the tubing. Easy to test, easy to fix, and worth checking before assuming the whole system is failing.

Bags and filters neglected

Running a bagged unit without a bag, or with a filter that has never been changed, chokes the motor and shortens its life considerably. We will show you what your unit takes and where to get them.

It came with the house

Very common. You bought a home with a central vac installed by someone else, no manual, no idea when it was last serviced or whether it still works properly. We will go through it with you.

What a Central Vacuum Service Covers

The cleaning

  • Tubing runs cleaned through from inlets back to the unit
  • Any blockage located and cleared
  • Canister emptied and cleaned out
  • Filter cleaned or replaced as the unit requires
  • Exhaust line checked where the unit vents outside

The check-over

  • Suction tested at every inlet, not just one
  • Inlet valves and low-voltage contacts checked
  • Motor condition and brush wear assessed
  • Hose and attachments inspected for splits
  • A straight answer on whether it is worth repairing or replacing

If your unit is genuinely at the end of its life we will say so rather than charging for a service that buys you six months. Motors are a wear item and there is a point where replacement is the sensible spend.
