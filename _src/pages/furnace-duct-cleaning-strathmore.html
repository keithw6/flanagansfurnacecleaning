Furnace and Duct Cleaning for Strathmore Homes

Strathmore sits about forty minutes east of Calgary on Highway 1, the main town in Wheatland County, surrounded on every side by cultivated land. The town itself has an older core going back decades, a substantial belt of 1980s through 2000s family housing, and newer subdivisions on the edges. Around it are farms, acreages and country residential.

The single biggest difference from a Calgary job is the dust load. Agricultural land generates a lot of airborne soil, especially at seeding and harvest, and there is nothing between the fields and the town to slow the wind carrying it. Add gravel approaches on rural properties and you get duct systems that load faster than anything inside the city.

Practically, that means we usually recommend a three year cycle here where we would say five for a quiet home in town. Not because we want the work more often, but because it is what actually comes out of these systems.

Farm and Acreage Properties

Grain and soil dust

Working farms produce fine particulate that town properties never see: grain dust at harvest, soil at seeding and cultivation, and whatever the wind carries the rest of the year. It gets into the house on clothing and boots and ends up in the cold air return.

Gravel approaches

A long gravel driveway generates grit every time a vehicle moves along it in dry weather. That grit tracks directly into the house, and the return side is where it accumulates.

Larger homes, longer runs

Farmhouses and acreage homes are frequently large, often extended over the years, and sometimes have two furnaces. Thirty to forty-five vents is common. Long horizontal runs mean airflow at the far end suffers first when the system loads.

Heated shops

Almost every farm and acreage has one, with its own unit heater or furnace, and they load quickly in a working environment with welding, grinding and vehicle traffic. We can quote the shop alongside the house, which is cheaper than a separate visit. Larger shops fall under commercial work.

Wood stoves and secondary heat

Common out here. A wood stove in regular use puts fine ash and smoke residue into the room air, which the return system pulls in and redistributes through the ductwork.

Filters that cannot cope

A thin builder-grade filter changed twice a year is not adequate in an agricultural environment. This is precisely the situation where a thicker media cabinet earns its cost, and we will tell you honestly whether one fits your setup.

In Town Strathmore

The town properties are a more familiar job. The older core has stock going back far enough that we occasionally find original ductwork with asbestos-wrapped sections, which we identify and leave alone because that is a licensed abatement job rather than a duct cleaning.

The 1980s through 2000s belt is ordinary accumulation work: loaded returns, furred blower wheels, developed basements that put drywall dust through the system. The newer subdivisions on the edges bring the modern package of sealed high-efficiency furnaces, HRVs, some flex duct on upper runs, and construction debris in anything built recently.

Even in town, though, the field dust reaches you. Strathmore properties generally suit a shorter interval than an equivalent house in Calgary would.
