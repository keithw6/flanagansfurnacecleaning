Furnace and Duct Cleaning for Langdon Homes

Langdon sits southeast of Chestermere in Rocky View County, a hamlet that has grown substantially over the last two decades while keeping the acreages and country residential around it. The housing splits into the older hamlet core, the newer subdivisions built since the 2000s, and the acreage properties spread across the surrounding land.

What unites them is the dust load. This is genuinely a different environment from a Calgary suburb, and duct systems out here reflect it. A house on a paved city street with a garage and a lawn gets a certain amount of soil tracked in. A house on an acreage with a gravel approach, surrounded by cultivated fields, gets several times that.

It is the main reason we tell Langdon customers that a three year cycle usually makes more sense than the five years we would suggest for a quiet home in town.

What Makes Rural Systems Different

Gravel and field dust

A gravel driveway generates fine grit every time a vehicle moves on it, and there is nothing between the surrounding fields and the house to slow the wind. Seeding and harvest both put a real quantity of soil into the air. It comes in on boots and through every opening.

Larger homes, longer runs

Acreage houses tend to be bigger than their town equivalents, often with a bonus room, a fully developed walkout, or a wing added later. That means high vent counts, thirty to forty-five is common, and duct runs long enough that airflow at the far end suffers noticeably once the system loads.

Two furnaces

Common in the larger acreage homes and in properties that have been extended. Two blowers, two filter racks, two trunk systems. A materially bigger job than a single-system house, and better established on the phone than discovered on the day.

Shops and outbuildings

Many acreages have a heated shop or garage with its own unit heater or furnace. Those load fast in a working shop environment. We can quote them alongside the house, and it is usually cheaper done in the same visit. See commercial work for larger shops.

Wood stoves and secondary heat

More common out here than in the city. A wood stove or fireplace in regular use puts fine ash and smoke residue into the room air, which the return system then pulls in and distributes through the ductwork.

Filters that cannot keep up

A thin builder-grade filter changed twice a year is simply not adequate in this environment. This is the situation where a thicker media cabinet genuinely earns its cost, and we will tell you honestly whether one will fit your setup.
