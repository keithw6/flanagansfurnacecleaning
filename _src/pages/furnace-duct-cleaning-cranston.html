Two Communities, Twenty Years Apart

Cranston proper, 2000 to 2012

The original build sits up on the ridge above the Bow, along Cranston Drive, Cranfield, Cranleigh and Cranarch. These homes are now fifteen to twenty-five years old, which puts them firmly into ordinary accumulation territory.

What we find is what we find in any Calgary house of that age: well loaded return ducts, a blower wheel furred with pet hair and fine dust, and very often the residue of a basement finished some years after possession.

Many of these are on their second owner. If nobody could tell you when the system was last cleaned, assume never.

Riverstone, 2014 onward

Down the escarpment toward the river, Riverstone is a completely different proposition. Homes are large, mostly under a decade old, and carrying construction debris rather than household dust.

Drywall dust is the main one. Add sawdust, insulation fibre, screws and poly offcuts. These builds also bring HRVs, flexible duct on some upper runs, sealed modulating furnaces with ECM blowers, and thin builder-grade filters that load fast.

If you took possession in the last year and your street is still finishing, call and ask before booking. We may tell you to wait a season.

What the Escarpment Does to Duct Systems

Cranston is built across a significant grade, and that shows up in the mechanical design more than people expect.

Walkout basements

Very common on the ridge and throughout Riverstone. A walkout changes the duct layout: the lower level has its own supply and return arrangement, and the return path is often less obvious than in a standard basement. We map the system before we start rather than assuming.

Three and four level runs

Walkout plus main plus upper plus bonus room means genuinely tall duct runs. More surface for dust to settle on and more distance for the blower to work against. Loading shows up first as weak airflow in the furthest upstairs room.

Larger homes, more vents

Cranston and especially Riverstone homes run big. Thirty to forty vents is normal and the larger Riverstone houses go higher. A rough count helps us quote properly, which is why we ask instead of working from square footage.

Two furnaces

Common in the larger homes, particularly where there is a bonus room over the garage or a fully finished walkout. Two blowers, two filter racks, two trunk systems. A bigger job than a single-system house, and better established on the phone than on the day.

Air conditioning

Widespread in both parts of the community. The evaporator coil sits in the airflow above the furnace and restricts air the same way a plugged filter does, so it is worth checking while we are in there.

Exposure to the valley

The ridge streets are open to the wind coming up the Bow valley. More airborne dust than a sheltered inner-city street gets, and shoulder-season open windows bring it in.
