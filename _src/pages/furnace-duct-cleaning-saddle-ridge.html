Furnace and Duct Cleaning for Saddle Ridge Homes

Saddle Ridge went up fast. A handful of streets were in the ground in the late 1990s, but the community as most people know it was built between about 2002 and 2012, filling in from Saddletowne Circle outward toward 80 Ave NE and the 60 Street edge. That means the overwhelming majority of houses here are now somewhere between fifteen and twenty-five years old.

That age bracket matters more than people expect. A furnace and duct system in a fifteen-year-old house has been running long enough to accumulate a real load of dust, but not so long that anyone has necessarily thought about it. A lot of our Saddle Ridge calls are from second owners who have just moved in, pulled a register cover off out of curiosity, and did not like what they found.

What we usually find here

  • Original builder ductwork, standard rectangular trunk with round branch lines
  • Heavy loading on the cold air return side, especially the basement returns
  • Drywall dust from a basement development done after possession
  • Blower wheels with a visible layer of fine grey dust and pet hair
  • Builder-grade one inch filters that have been left in far too long
  • Bathroom and kitchen exhaust runs that have never been touched

Why the return side loads faster in Saddle Ridge

Saddle Ridge has a high proportion of multi-generational households. More people living in the home means more cooking, more laundry, more foot traffic and more time with the furnace running, and all of that ends up on the return air side of the system.

Homes where someone cooks daily at high heat also push a fine grease aerosol into the return, which is stickier than ordinary dust and holds onto everything that lands on it. It is not a problem, it is just a faster cycle. Those homes benefit from a cleaning every three years rather than five, and a filter change every two to three months instead of six.

What Saddle Ridge Housing Stock Means for Your Ductwork

Developed basements

Saddle Ridge has an unusually high rate of basement development, both legal secondary suites and family space. If the furnace was running while drywall was being sanded, and it almost always was, that dust went straight into the return and settled through the entire system. This is the single most common reason we get called out here.

Added suite ductwork

Suited basements often have branch lines added after the original build, sometimes flex, sometimes tapped into the existing trunk. Those additions are frequently the dirtiest runs in the house because they were installed into a system that was already in service. We clean them the same as everything else.

Rear-lane garages and entry dust

Much of Saddle Ridge is rear-lane with detached garages, so people track in across a gravel lane and a back yard rather than off a paved front driveway. More grit at the back door means more grit in the return.

High-efficiency furnaces

Almost every furnace here is a condensing high-efficiency unit with a sealed combustion chamber and a variable or multi-speed blower. These are good furnaces, but the tighter tolerances mean a dirty blower wheel affects airflow more noticeably than it would on an old mid-efficiency unit.

Filter racks that fight you

Several builders in this community used a filter slot behind the return plenum that is genuinely awkward to reach. We see filters that have not been changed in two years for no other reason than that. We show you where yours is and how to get it out.

Airport proximity

The east side of Saddle Ridge sits under the approach to the Calgary airport and beside a lot of open land. Spring and fall wind carries a steady amount of fine prairie dust, and windows open in shoulder season brings it inside.
