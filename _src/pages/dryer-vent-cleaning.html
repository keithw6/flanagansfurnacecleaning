Why Dryer Vents Matter More Than People Think

A clothes dryer works by pushing hot air through wet fabric and then blowing that hot, damp, lint-laden air outside. The lint screen catches the coarse stuff. The fine lint goes down the pipe, and because the air is damp it sticks to the walls rather than blowing clear.

Over a few years that coating gets thick enough to narrow the pipe. The dryer takes longer to finish a load, so it runs longer, so it pushes more heat into a pipe that is now lined with the most flammable material in your house. That is the whole mechanism, and it is why dryers are a routine cause of residential fires.

Signs yours needs doing

  • Clothes need two cycles to dry
  • The dryer or the laundry room gets noticeably hot
  • A burning or hot-lint smell during a cycle
  • Lint visible around the outside hood
  • The outside flap does not open fully when running
  • You have never had it done

Calgary specifics

Cold weather makes it worse. Warm damp air hitting a freezing pipe condenses, and damp lint packs down harder than dry lint. Exterior hoods also ice up in a deep cold snap, which throttles airflow further and forces the dryer to run longer still.

Birds nesting in the hood over summer is common on ground-floor terminations, particularly in older communities with mature trees.

The runs that are worst

Anything long. Laundry on an upper floor venting through the roof, or a basement laundry running the full width of the house to reach an exterior wall, gives lint far more surface to collect on and far more bends to catch at.

Flexible foil or vinyl transition hose behind the dryer is the single worst offender. If yours has one, we will tell you what to replace it with.

How We Clean a Dryer Vent

  1. Pull the dryer out

    We disconnect it and check the transition hose behind it, which is where a surprising amount of the lint actually sits.

  2. Brush the full run

    A rotating brush on a flexible rod goes the whole length of the pipe, not just as far as an arm reaches. Every bend gets worked.

  3. Vacuum as we go

    Under suction so the lint comes out rather than getting pushed further along or into the room.

  4. Clear the exterior hood

    From outside as well as inside. The flap and the screen behind it are usually where the worst of the packing is.

  5. Reconnect and test

    Dryer back in place, run a cycle, and check the airflow at the outside hood so you can feel the difference.

Red shows where lint accumulates. Airflow drops, the dryer runs longer, and the pipe gets hotter.
