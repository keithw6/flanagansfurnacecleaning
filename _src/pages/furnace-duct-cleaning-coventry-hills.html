Furnace and Duct Cleaning for Coventry Hills Homes

Coventry Hills went up between roughly 1995 and 2007, filling the area north of Country Hills Boulevard around Coventry Hills Way and Covebrook, out toward Harvest Hills and the Country Hills Village retail area. The oldest homes are approaching thirty years and even the newest are pushing twenty.

That age changes what the job is. In Redstone or Cornerstone we are removing construction debris. Here we are removing two to three decades of genuine household accumulation: skin cells, fabric fibre, pet hair, cooking residue, soil tracked in from outside, and whatever a basement development contributed along the way.

It also means a lot of these homes are on their second owner, sometimes their third. If you bought and nobody could tell you when the system was last cleaned, the honest assumption is never.

What Twenty-Five Years of Loading Looks Like

Furred blower wheels

The single most striking thing in homes of this age. A blower wheel has curved fins, and pet hair and fine dust pack into the gaps between them until the wheel is effectively a solid drum. It moves noticeably less air, and almost nobody has ever seen theirs.

Original ductwork, well loaded

Standard rectangular trunk with round branch lines, which is straightforward to clean properly. The volume of material that comes out of a system this age is what surprises people, and it is the reason we show you rather than describe it.

Basement developments

Most Coventry Hills basements were developed at some point after possession, often in the 2000s. Drywall sanding with the furnace running put a large volume of fine dust through the return side, and it is still in there unless someone has cleaned it out since.

Aging humidifiers

Nearly every home here has a furnace humidifier and a large share of them are not working. Scaled-solid pads, water feeds shut off years ago and forgotten, or the bypass damper left in the summer position permanently. We check and can replace a pad while we are there.

Mid-efficiency and early high-efficiency

Original furnaces from this era are largely gone, replaced somewhere along the line. What we see now is a mix of early condensing units and more recent replacements, sometimes with the replacement fitted into ductwork sized for the old unit.

Filter racks nobody uses

Twenty-five year old filter slots that are awkward to reach, with a filter in them that has been there far longer than it should. We will show you where yours is and how to get it out without a fight.

Just Bought in Coventry Hills?

A large share of our work here is new owners in the first few months. It is a sensible time to do it and the reasoning is straightforward.

You have no history on the system. The previous owner may have had it cleaned regularly, or never. If they had pets and you do not, or you have pets and they did not, the system is carrying somebody else's household. And if anyone smoked in the house at any point, that residue is in the ductwork and it re-releases every time the blower runs.

Doing it once, properly, at the start gives you a known baseline. After that a three to five year cycle is fine for most households. The combined furnace and duct cleaning is what we would suggest for a first pass, since you want the blower dealt with as well as the pipe.
