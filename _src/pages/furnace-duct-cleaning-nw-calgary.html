What We Find in Northwest Systems

Older inner-city homes

Hillhurst, Sunnyside, Capitol Hill, Banff Trail and the surrounding streets have housing going back a century. Systems have often been converted more than once: coal to oil to gas, gravity to forced air, with the original oversized trunk still in place feeding branches added at various points since.

Two things matter here. First, some very old systems have asbestos-wrapped duct sections. We identify those before touching anything and will not disturb them, because that is a licensed abatement job and not a duct cleaning. Second, a hundred years of accumulation in an original trunk is a genuinely different volume of material to shift.

Infill and laneway builds sitting between these older homes are the opposite problem: brand new, and full of construction dust.

The hillside and ridge communities

Tuscany, Scenic Acres, Edgemont, Hawkwood, Sherwood, Nolan Hill and Evanston are built across slopes, which produces walkout basements, split levels and multi-storey duct runs longer than a flat-lot house of the same square footage.

Longer vertical runs mean more surface for dust to settle on and more distance for the blower to push air through, so a partially loaded system shows up as poor performance in the far rooms sooner than it would elsewhere.

These communities also sit high and exposed. Wind off the open land to the north and west carries fine dust, and shoulder-season open windows bring it in.

One pattern that runs across the whole quadrant: this is where a lot of Calgary's larger family homes are, and larger homes have more vents. Thirty to forty is normal in the bigger Tuscany, Edgemont and Evanston houses. That affects the quote, which is why we ask for a rough count rather than working from square footage alone.
