What Makes NE Calgary Ductwork Different

The northeast is not one thing. It runs from post-war inner-city stock through the big 1990s and 2000s expansion out to communities still being framed today. Three broad eras, three different jobs.

Pre-1980 inner northeast

Marlborough, Rundle, Vista Heights, Mayland Heights and the like. Larger trunks, sometimes original octopus layouts, occasionally asbestos-wrapped sections on very old systems. We identify wrapping before starting and will not disturb it, because that is an abatement job and not a duct cleaning.

1995 to 2012 expansion

Saddle Ridge, Taradale, Martindale, Coventry Hills, Panorama Hills. Standard rectangular trunk with round branches, fifteen to thirty years of loading, and very often a basement developed some years after possession which put drywall dust through the whole return side.

2013 onward

Redstone, Cornerstone, Cityscape, the newer parts of Skyview Ranch. Sealed high-efficiency furnaces, ECM blowers, HRVs as standard, flexible duct on some final runs, and construction debris the builder never removed.

Three things that show up across the whole quadrant

  • Developed basements. The northeast has a high rate of basement development, both suites and family space. Drywall sanding with the furnace running is the single most common reason we get called out here.
  • Higher household occupancy. Multi-generational households are common across much of the northeast. More people means more cooking, laundry and foot traffic, and it all loads the return air side faster.
  • Open land and wind. The north and east edges back onto open prairie and the airport lands. Spring and fall wind carries a steady supply of fine dust, and shoulder-season open windows bring it inside.

Why Being Based Here Matters

Two practical reasons, neither of which is about pricing since our pricing is flat citywide anyway.

The first is scheduling. A company crossing from the southwest has to give you a wide arrival window because it is guessing at Deerfoot. We can usually commit to something tighter for northeast jobs, and if we are running late we are running late by fifteen minutes rather than an hour.

The second is follow-up. If something needs a second look, or you have a question a week later, a return trip is a ten minute drive rather than a half-day expedition. That changes how willing a company is to come back, and it is worth knowing which kind you have hired.
