Furnace and Duct Cleaning for Mahogany Homes

Mahogany started going in around 2008 and has been under continuous construction ever since, phase after phase working outward from the lake toward 52 Street and Stoney Trail. Some of the earliest streets are approaching twenty years old now. Plenty of houses in the newer phases took possession within the last two or three years. Westman Village adds a large multi-family component on top of that.

That spread means we get two very different calls from this community, and they need different conversations.

Recent possessions, under five years

These systems are mechanically clean but full of construction debris. Drywall dust is the main one, and it is fine enough to coat the entire interior of the ductwork. We also routinely pull out sawdust, insulation fibre, drywall screws, snapped-off zip ties, offcuts of poly, coffee cups and, more than once, a tape measure.

Builders often say the ducts were cleaned at possession. What usually happened is that the register openings were vacuumed so they looked clean for the walkthrough. That is not the same job.

Original phases, ten to eighteen years

The streets closer to Mahogany Lake and the original Mahogany Boulevard sections are now old enough to be on a normal maintenance cycle. Construction debris is long gone, replaced by ordinary household dust, pet hair on the blower wheel, and whatever a finished basement contributed.

These are straightforward jobs and priced the same as any comparable home elsewhere in the city.

What Mahogany Housing Stock Means for Your Ductwork

Flex duct on the final runs

Several builders working in Mahogany use insulated flexible duct for the last stretch to upper-floor registers rather than running rigid pipe the whole way. Flex has a ribbed interior that holds dust far better than smooth metal, and it tears if somebody puts an aggressive rotary brush down it. It needs a softer brush head and more patience. We check what you have before we start.

HRVs and fresh air intakes

Newer Alberta code brought heat recovery ventilators into most of these builds. An HRV has its own duct network, its own core and its own pair of filters, entirely separate from the furnace. A lot of homeowners here do not know they have one, and have never cleaned or replaced the core filters. It is worth asking us to look at it while we are there.

Sealed high-efficiency furnaces

Two-stage and modulating condensing furnaces with ECM blower motors are standard in Mahogany. They are efficient and quiet, and they are also more sensitive to restricted airflow than the older equipment they replaced. A dust-loaded blower wheel or a plugged filter will show up as poor performance faster on one of these than it would on a twenty-year-old mid-efficiency unit.

Builder-grade one inch filters

Almost every home here came with a thin one inch filter in a slot beside the furnace. They are cheap and they clog quickly, and during the first two years of a new home they clog even faster because of residual construction dust. Change them every two to three months for the first couple of years.

Ongoing construction next door

If your street is still being built out, dust is arriving continuously from framing, cutting, grading and street sweeping. It comes in on shoes, through open windows in summer, and through the fresh air intake. Cleaning too early in a phase means doing it again, and we will tell you honestly if it is worth waiting a season.

Air conditioning

Far more Mahogany homes have air conditioning than older Calgary stock does. That means the system runs in summer too, and the evaporator coil sits directly in the airflow above the furnace. A dirty coil restricts air the same way a dirty filter does, and it is worth a look during a cleaning.

When to Book a New Mahogany Home

The question we get most from this community is when a brand-new house should have its first cleaning. Our honest answer is roughly a year after possession, and after any basement development is finished, whichever comes later.

Cleaning the week you move in feels satisfying but it is usually premature. Trades will still be back for warranty work, the basement may not be developed yet, and if construction is ongoing on your street the system will reload. Waiting until the first year is behind you means one cleaning does the whole job instead of two doing half of it each. That advice costs us a booking and we give it anyway, because the alternative is you paying us twice.

The exception is if someone in the house has asthma or significant allergies, or if there is a newborn. In that case, book it as soon as the trades are done and accept that a second cleaning may be worthwhile later.
