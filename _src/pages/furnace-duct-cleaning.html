Why We Recommend Doing Both

People usually call asking for one or the other, and the honest answer is that doing just one is half a job in most cases.

Clean the ductwork but leave the furnace alone, and the blower wheel is still carrying its layer of dust and pet hair. The first time it fires up it starts feeding that back into freshly cleaned pipe. Clean the furnace but leave the ducts, and the unit pulls decades of settled dust off the duct walls within a few heating cycles.

The two jobs also overlap physically. We are already at the furnace, already have the vacuum connected at the trunk, already have the equipment set up in your mechanical room. Doing both at once takes maybe forty minutes longer than doing one, which is why the combined price is nowhere near double.

When one alone makes sense

If you had the full system done two or three years ago and only want the furnace unit checked and cleaned before winter, that is a reasonable ask and we will quote it on its own.

Same in reverse: a brand-new home where the furnace is spotless but the ducts are full of drywall dust from construction only needs the duct side. We will say so rather than sell you both.

When both is the only sensible option

A home that has never been done. A house you have just bought where you have no history. A basement development finished in the last year. Any system where you can see fuzz around the register grilles.

In those cases doing one side only means paying us again sooner than you should have to.

What the Combined Service Covers

The furnace side

  • Blower compartment opened and vacuumed
  • Blower wheel cleaned, fins individually where access allows
  • Burner compartment cleaned out
  • Heat exchanger surfaces cleaned and visually inspected
  • Filter rack cleaned and a new standard filter fitted
  • Condensate drain checked on high-efficiency units

The duct side

  • Main supply trunk brushed and vacuumed end to end
  • Every supply branch line worked individually
  • Every cold air return line back to the furnace
  • All registers and grilles removed, cleaned and refitted
  • Return air plenum and furnace-side connections
  • Any added suite or basement ductwork

A typical house takes two to four hours. Larger two-storey homes with a developed basement and thirty-plus vents sit at the longer end. We do not compress a job to fit another one in behind it.

Add-Ons, and the Ones We Will Talk You Out Of

There are genuine extras worth doing at the same visit, and there are extras the industry sells because they are profitable. We will tell you which is which.

Worth adding while we are there

  • Dryer vent cleaning, genuinely a fire risk and almost never done
  • Bathroom and kitchen exhaust runs, usually original and never touched
  • Furnace humidifier pad replacement if yours has one
  • A better filter if your rack takes one, which we will show you

Usually not worth it

  • Blanket sanitizing on a system with no actual moisture or mould problem
  • Fogging deodorizers, which mask rather than remove
  • UV lights sold as a cure for dusty ducts
  • Annual cleaning on a two-person household with no pets

When sanitizing is warranted is a real conversation, and we do offer it. We just do not sell it by default.
