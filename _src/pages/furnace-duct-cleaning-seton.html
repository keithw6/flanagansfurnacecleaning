Furnace and Duct Cleaning for Seton Homes

Seton began around 2014 as the urban district anchoring the deep southeast, built around the South Health Campus with the YMCA, the shopping district and a genuine town-centre density that is unusual for a new Calgary community. It is still going up.

Housing here skews to apartment condos, townhome blocks, semi-detached and laned product, with fewer large detached homes than neighbouring Mahogany or Cranston. That means smaller systems, fewer vents and tighter mechanical rooms, and it means the job should cost less than a two-storey detached house. Some companies quote one flat residential rate regardless. We do not.

What is in these systems is construction material rather than household dust. Drywall dust is the main one, fine enough to coat every interior surface, and it does not move on its own. Sawdust, insulation fibre, screws and poly offcuts turn up regularly too.

The Timing Question in Seton

This community is where we most often advise waiting, alongside Cornerstone in the northeast, and the reasoning is worth setting out.

While your block is still under construction, dust is arriving continuously. Framing, cutting, grading and street sweeping generate fine particulate that comes into your home on shoes, through open windows in summer, and through the fresh air intake on your HRV. Cleaning a system in the middle of that means paying to do it again later.

Wait if

  • Buildings or lots nearby are still going up
  • You took possession within the last year
  • Trades are still returning for warranty work
  • Your basement, if you have one, is not yet developed

Book it now if

  • Your block is finished and the surrounding streets are quiet
  • You have been in two or more years and never had it done
  • You are a second owner with no history on the system
  • Someone in the home has asthma, significant allergies, or there is a newborn

Call, describe where your block is at, and we will tell you honestly. If the answer is wait another season, that is what you will hear.

What Seton Systems Look Like

Apartment condo units

Compact furnace or air handler in a utility closet feeding a handful of vents. Quick and inexpensive. Worth knowing: the building's makeup air and corridor pressurisation systems are the condo corporation's responsibility, not yours. We will tell you where that line falls.

Townhome blocks

Eight to fourteen vents, closet furnace, short vertical runs. If several neighbours want to coordinate, doing multiple units in one visit is more efficient and priced accordingly.

HRVs throughout

Standard in every build of this era. Own ducting, a core and two filters. In a townhome or condo the HRV is usually in an awkward corner and almost never opened. Very likely running below rated airflow if it has never been serviced.

Sealed modulating furnaces

High-efficiency condensing units with ECM blowers. Sensitive to restricted airflow, so a blower carrying construction dust or a plugged filter shows up as a performance issue sooner than on older equipment.

Thin builder filters

One inch filters that clog fast, and faster still in a new build with residual construction dust. Every two to three months for the first couple of years.

Rentals and investor units

A significant share of Seton is rented. Between tenancies is the natural time, and dryer vent cleaning is worth adding since it matters a great deal if there is ever an insurance question.
