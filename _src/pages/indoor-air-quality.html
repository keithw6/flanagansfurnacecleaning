What Actually Affects the Air in a Calgary Home

Calgary has a specific set of conditions. Very dry air most of the year, a long heating season with the house sealed up, chinooks that swing temperature and pressure, wildfire smoke in some summers, and a lot of new construction throwing dust into the air on the edges of the city.

Against that, most homes are running a one inch filter that costs a few dollars and gets changed when someone remembers. There is usually a lot of easy improvement available before anyone needs to spend money on equipment.

Filtration

The single biggest lever, and the cheapest. What matters is not just the filter rating but whether your system can move air through it, and whether it is actually sealed in the rack rather than letting air bypass around the edges.

Humidity

Calgary winters run dry enough to crack furniture and dry out sinuses. A furnace humidifier helps a lot when it is working, and most are not. Equally, too much humidity causes window condensation and is worth dialling back.

Ventilation

Newer airtight homes need mechanical fresh air. If you have an HRV, its filters and core need cleaning, and a lot of owners have never touched theirs. Older leaky homes ventilate themselves, for better and worse.

Source removal

Cleaning the ductwork removes the reservoir of dust that keeps recirculating. It is not a cure for allergies but it stops the system redistributing what has built up in it.

Wildfire smoke

During bad smoke events the useful moves are keeping windows shut, running the furnace fan continuously with a decent filter, and a portable HEPA unit in the bedroom. Cheap and effective.

What you bring in

Shoes, pets, cooking and hobbies contribute more than most people expect. Not something we sell a fix for, but worth knowing before buying equipment.

Furnace Filtration Systems

The filter is where most people should start, and where most people go wrong in one of two directions.

Too cheap

The fibreglass panel filters sold as furnace filters are really there to stop large debris reaching the blower. They do very little for the fine particulate that affects how a house feels. If that is what is in your rack, moving up a grade is a cheap improvement.

They also need changing every one to three months, and almost nobody does.

Too restrictive

The opposite mistake, and more damaging. Fitting the highest-rated one inch filter you can buy into a system not designed for it starves the furnace of air. That means longer run times, a strained blower, and on some units a high-limit shutdown.

If you want serious filtration, the answer is a thicker media cabinet with more surface area, not a denser thin filter.

A four or five inch media cabinet is the upgrade that genuinely works for most Calgary homes. More filter surface means better filtration and less resistance at the same time, and the filter only needs changing once or twice a year. It requires space beside the furnace and a modification to the ductwork, so it is not right for every mechanical room.

When we are at your house for a furnace and duct cleaning we will show you what your rack takes, tell you whether a media cabinet would fit, and give you a straight opinion on whether it is worth it for your situation.

Humidifiers and HRVs

Furnace humidifiers

Most Calgary homes have one. A large share of them are not working, usually because the pad is scaled solid, the water feed is shut off, or the bypass damper is in the summer position year round.

Target humidity in a Calgary winter is roughly 30 to 40 percent, lower in a deep cold snap. Higher than that and you get condensation running down the windows and pooling on sills, which causes its own problems.

We check and service humidifiers as part of our work and can replace a pad while we are there.

Heat recovery ventilators

Standard in newer builds. An HRV brings in fresh outside air and exhausts stale inside air, passing them through a core so the outgoing air pre-warms the incoming air.

They have two filters and a core, all of which need periodic cleaning. Owners in newer communities like Mahogany, Seton, Redstone and Cornerstone frequently do not know they have one.

If yours has never been serviced, it is very likely running at a fraction of its rated airflow.

What We Will Not Sell You

  • UV lights marketed as a fix for dusty ducts. UV has legitimate uses on wet coil surfaces; it does nothing to airborne dust travelling past it at speed.
  • Ozone generators. Ozone is a lung irritant and is not something to run in an occupied house.
  • Electronic air cleaners for a household with no specific need. They need regular washing and most owners stop doing it within a year.
  • Duct sanitizing as an allergy treatment on a dry system with no moisture problem.
  • Any equipment at all before the ductwork has been cleaned and the filter situation sorted out. Those two come first and often make the rest unnecessary.
