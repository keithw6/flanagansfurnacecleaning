Furnace and Duct Cleaning for Redstone Homes

Redstone started going in around 2013 and filled out quickly, spreading north and east from Country Hills Boulevard toward Stoney Trail and the airport lands. The earliest streets are now a decade old. Some of the outer phases took possession within the last two or three years.

What that means practically is that we almost never find an age problem here. We find a build problem. Drywall dust is the constant, fine enough to coat the entire interior of a duct system and invisible until you pull a register and look. Beyond that we regularly pull out sawdust, insulation fibre, drywall screws, snapped zip ties, offcuts of poly vapour barrier, drink cans and the occasional tool.

Builders frequently state that the ducts were cleaned before possession. What usually happened is that the visible register openings were vacuumed so they looked right at the walkthrough. That is not the same job as cleaning a duct system, and the difference is obvious the moment a brush goes down a branch line.

What Redstone Housing Stock Means for Your Ductwork

A mix of house types

Redstone has laned homes, front-garage detached, semi-detached and townhome blocks in close proximity. The laned and semi-detached homes are narrow, so duct runs go up rather than out, and mechanical rooms are tight. That does not change the price but it does change how we set up.

Flexible duct on upper runs

Several builders working here used insulated flex for the final stretch to upper-floor registers instead of running rigid pipe all the way. Flex has a ribbed interior that holds dust far better than smooth metal, and an aggressive rotary brush will tear it. It needs a softer head and more patience.

HRVs nobody knows about

Newer Alberta code brought heat recovery ventilators into most of these builds. An HRV has its own duct network, its own core and two filters, completely separate from the furnace. Plenty of Redstone owners have never opened theirs. Worth asking us to look while we are there.

Sealed high-efficiency furnaces

Two-stage or modulating condensing units with ECM blower motors are standard. Efficient and quiet, but more sensitive to restricted airflow than the older equipment they replaced. A dust-loaded blower wheel or a plugged filter shows up as poor performance sooner.

Thin builder filters

Nearly every home came with a one inch filter in a slot beside the furnace. They clog quickly, and in the first two years of a new home they clog faster still because of residual construction dust. Change them every two to three months early on.

Basements developed later

A lot of Redstone buyers took possession with an undeveloped basement and finished it a few years in. If the furnace ran during the drywall stage, and it usually did, that dust went straight through the return side and settled through the whole system.

When a Redstone Home Is Actually Ready for Cleaning

Our advice on new builds costs us bookings and we give it anyway: wait about a year after possession, and until any basement development is finished, whichever comes later.

Cleaning in your first month feels satisfying but trades will still be back for warranty work, the basement may be unfinished, and if your street or the phase next to you is still being framed the system will simply reload. One cleaning after that first year does the whole job rather than two doing half each.

The exception is asthma, serious allergies or a newborn. In that case book it once the trades are done and accept that a second cleaning later may be worthwhile.

If you bought in Redstone's earliest phases around 2013 to 2016 and have never had the system done, you are past that window and it is genuinely due.
